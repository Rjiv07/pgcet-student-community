# Sprint 003 — Community Knowledge

## Product decision
The website should complement Reddit and Telegram, not force users to leave those platforms or copy entire conversations into a separate feed.

## Experience
The Community page explains a natural editorial loop:
1. Students discuss questions in existing community spaces.
2. A moderator/editor identifies a useful recurring question and prepares a human-reviewed summary.
3. The summary links to the original conversation, records its review date, distinguishes experience/opinion from verified facts, and points to official sources where needed.
4. When appropriate, the useful answer becomes a lasting guide or resource rather than an unstructured stream of copied comments.

## Safety and integrity
- No automated Reddit scraping or bulk republication is implemented.
- Link to the original discussion and respect the platform's current terms and attribution rules.
- Obtain permission before reproducing identifiable user quotes or private content.
- Never present a student's personal experience as official policy.
- Keep current exam/admissions claims linked to authoritative sources and review dates.
- Do not create sample discussions, fake quotes, or engagement statistics to fill the page.

## Scope
This sprint adds the editorial explanation and styling only. The initial site remains static-first; it does not yet have a moderation dashboard, submission database, or automated summarisation pipeline.
